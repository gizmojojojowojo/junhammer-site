## The problem

AI data centers need land, power, fiber and permits in the same place, and the evidence for each lives in different systems: utility maps, county records, transportation data and policy documents. Early-stage investors and developers spend weeks assembling it, and comparisons between sites are rarely like for like.

## What I built

LandmAIn turns that scattered evidence into a disciplined, repeatable screen.

- **One evidence model.** Each opportunity is scored against the same criteria: transmission and substation proximity, highway access, fiber evidence, land characteristics and policy context.
- **Sources you can check.** Every finding carries a source date and confidence language, so readers can see what is established and what still needs diligence.
- **Clear next steps.** Map signals become specific diligence questions: utility capacity, carrier service, entitlement, water, buildable acreage and site control.
- **Delivery formats people use.** An interactive corridor explorer, a GIS map atlas, standardized opportunity profiles and an executive briefing deck.

The public demonstration runs as a fast static site on Cloudflare. The source project and full screening inventory stay private.

## What it shows

LandmAIn is the kind of AI work I like best: a narrow, high-value question; messy public data; and an output that makes its evidence boundary explicit instead of overselling certainty. The same approach applies to research tools for marketing, healthcare and operations teams.
