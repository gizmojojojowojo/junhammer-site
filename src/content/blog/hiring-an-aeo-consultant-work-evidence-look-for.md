An AEO consultant helps a business improve how its information appears in AI answers. The role can include customer-question research, technical reviews, content strategy, implementation guidance, and measurement. The right scope depends on the business, the website, and the people available to carry out the work.When evaluating an AEO consultant, start with the decisions you need help making. You might need someone to diagnose a visibility problem, improve a group of service pages, establish measurement, or direct an internal team.

Those are concrete assignments. They give you a way to assess experience and compare proposals.Inspect the evidence carefully. A relevant case study should show the starting point, the work, the result, and the period measured. A reporting example should explain exactly what its numbers represent. A consultant who can walk through those details gives you something solid to evaluate.

## Decide which role you need

An AEO consultant can serve as a diagnostician, a strategist, an implementation partner, or a program lead. Identify the role in the brief so the consultant can explain how they will work with your team. If you have capable writers and developers, you may need research, prioritization, and technical review. If the business lacks those resources, the engagement needs a practical route to implementation. Write down the work your team can already handle. Include publishing, development, specialist review, analytics, and access to customer information. Then identify where progress gets stuck. A useful brief might read: “We need to understand why our implementation services receive little relevant search exposure. Our team can revise and publish content. We need research, a prioritized page plan, and a measurement process.” That tells the consultant where their judgment is required. It also helps you evaluate whether their proposed staffing and deliverables match the assignment.

## Ask the consultant to explain one real decision

During the evaluation, choose one existing page and ask how the consultant would assess it. Give them enough business context to make the discussion meaningful. Listen for a sequence of decisions. Which buyer question does the page answer? What information is missing? Which statements need evidence? What would they check technically? How would they prioritize this page against other work? Google's guidance on hiring an SEO recommends asking about previous work, relevant experience, expected results, and measurement. Those questions are useful foundations for this conversation.

## Read case studies closely

A case study is most useful when the reader can reconstruct the comparison. It should identify the metric, the starting value, the ending value, the dates, and the scope of the work. Those details let you judge what the example demonstrates. One healthcare SEO project in my portfolio provides a concrete example. The published case study reports clicks increasing from 2.74K to 39.4K between the first quarter of 2022 and the first quarter of 2023. Impressions increased from 339K to 2.25 million over those matching quarters.

## Check the measurement plan before the engagement

An AEO measurement plan should specify the questions being tested, the platforms involved, the observation schedule, and the way results will be classified. It should also explain how the team will use available platform data and business records. For manual or automated answer checks, request a sample log. A useful record includes the exact question, test date, relevant settings, response, cited URLs, and an accuracy review. Decide what counts as a mention and what counts as a citation. If the business is named without a link, record that explicitly. If a page is linked as supporting evidence, save the destination. If the answer describes the wrong service, flag the error. ## Look for technical judgment you can understand

Technical judgment becomes visible in the explanation of a problem. A consultant should identify the affected page, the evidence, the proposed change, and the expected practical effect. Ask them to walk through how they would investigate a page that receives little relevant exposure. The discussion should connect the technical review to the page's purpose and the information it contains. Ask where the recommendation comes from. Is it documented platform guidance, a result from their own test, or a working hypothesis? Each can contribute to a plan when it is labeled clearly. Google advises evaluating third-party SEO claims against its official guidance and distinguishing evidence-backed opinion from statements about its systems.

. For your team, the deliverable should be actionable. A developer needs reproduction steps and acceptance criteria. A writer needs the question, factual inputs, and intended reader. A manager needs the priority and dependencies. Clear instructions make it possible to evaluate whether the recommendation was carried out correctly.

## Give the consultant access to the right knowledge

An AEO consultant needs more than website access. They need a route to the information that makes your business useful to customers. Identify the people who answer detailed questions during sales and delivery. Ask them which issues repeatedly affect a purchase, a quote, or a project plan. Gather examples that can be checked against records. For a service business, this could include prerequisites, approval steps, common scope changes, and the conditions that affect timelines. For a product business, it could include compatibility, setup requirements, testing procedures, and support limits. The consultant's role is to turn that material into a research and publishing plan. Assign a subject expert to review the facts before publication. Keep the review focused enough that it fits into their normal workload. The initial plan should name at least one original information asset. A focused analysis of completed work gives the content program a source your business can maintain and expand.

## Put responsibilities and costs in writing

A consulting proposal should state who will research, write, review, implement, and measure the work. It should identify the recurring outputs and the decisions required from the client. Use a simple responsibility table in the scope:

| Work | Required output | Owner to name |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Questions, sources, and priorities | Consultant |
| Business facts | Approved factual brief | Client specialist |
| Content | Revised pages and review record | Assigned writer and editor |
| Technical changes | Implemented fixes and verification | Developer or implementation partner |
| Measurement | Baseline, follow-up, interpretation | Consultant and analytics owner |

Then connect the fee to the actual workload. Ask how many pages, interviews, research assets, and reporting cycles are included. Record how additional work will be priced. Account for internal time as well. A proposal that depends on unavailable developers or delayed expert reviews will need a revised schedule. Resolve those dependencies before choosing the start date. The final agreement should also explain what your team receives at the end: research files, content, measurement records, and the remaining work plan.

## Use the first review to make a decision

At the first formal review, ask the consultant to connect the business objective to the completed work. They should show which questions were prioritized, which pages changed, what evidence was added, and what the measurements indicate. Choose the next step based on that record. You might expand a topic that is attracting relevant inquiries. You might improve a page that receives exposure but leaves key buying questions unanswered. You might collect more data before changing direction. A useful AEO consultant helps the business make those decisions with clearer information. The relationship should leave your team better able to understand its search performance and maintain the work. If you are considering an engagement, start with one service you want to grow, a few pages that explain it, and the questions prospects ask before buying. Those materials are enough to begin a specific conversation about scope, priorities, and evidence.
