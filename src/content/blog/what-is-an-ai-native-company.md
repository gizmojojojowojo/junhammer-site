**Key takeaways**

- An **AI-native company** is built so AI does core work. Gartner describes an AI-native business model as [embedding AI at the core of how a business creates, delivers and captures value](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/ai-native).
- The simple test: switch the AI off. If important work stops, the company is AI-native in that area. If work only slows down, AI is assisting.
- There is no agreed definition yet. Splunk notes the industry [has yet to agree on a universally accepted definition of AI Native](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/learn/ai-native).
- Experts disagree on whether an existing company can become AI-native. [Cognizant says it cannot](https://www.cognizant.com/us/en/aem-i/how-to-think-and-act-like-an-ai-native-business). [Thoughtworks says AI-first is the realistic goal](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-au/insights/articles/path-to-ai-first-organization) for established firms.
- My answer: the label belongs to companies born around AI, but an established business can rebuild its operations so they run the same way, one workflow at a time.
- In McKinsey's interviews with AI-native companies, [the bottleneck was access to company knowledge, not the AI model](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-seven-operating-truths-of-AI-native-companies).

"AI-native company" is one of the phrases of 2026. Investors use it to sort startups. Consulting firms use it to sell transformation programs. If you run an established business, you may wonder whether it applies to you at all. This post gives a plain definition, a test you can apply to your own company, and an honest answer to the question most articles skip: can a business that already exists become AI-native?

## What is an AI-native company?

An AI-native company is one whose products, workflows and operating model are designed around AI, so AI does core work rather than assisting with it. Remove the AI and important parts of the business stop.

The big definitions agree on that core idea:

- **Gartner:** an AI-native business model means [embedding AI at the core of how a business creates, delivers and captures value](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/ai-native).
- **IBM:** AI native refers to [a product, company or workflow designed from the ground up](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-native) with AI as a core component, not added later as a feature.
- **Thoughtworks:** an AI-native organization is one whose [products, operating model and technology foundations have been designed around AI from the outset](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-au/insights/articles/path-to-ai-first-organization).

Notice that IBM includes workflows, not only products. A company does not need to sell AI to be AI-native. It can sell accounting, roofing or healthcare and still run its operations on AI.

Expect the term to change. IBM's own explainer predicts that [the term's usefulness will fade as AI becomes ubiquitous](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-native). The idea underneath it will last longer than the label: work designed around what AI can do.

## How can you tell if a company is AI-native?

Switch the AI off in your head and ask what happens. If whole functions stop, that part of the company is AI-native. If people simply slow down, AI is assisting them.

Three signs show up in companies that pass the test.

### Workflows are designed around AI

The process was built assuming AI does certain steps. Leads are sorted and routed by AI. Reports are generated, then reviewed. People handle exceptions and decisions instead of the routine work.

### The company's knowledge is written down and reachable

AI can only use what it can find. McKinsey interviewed 15 AI-centric companies and named this one of its seven operating truths: [your model isn't the bottleneck; accessing tribal knowledge is](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-seven-operating-truths-of-AI-native-companies). AI-native companies record their processes, decisions and data in places their AI systems can reach.

### Trust is earned and measured

AI gets more independence as it proves itself. McKinsey's companies follow the rule that [trust precedes autonomy](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-seven-operating-truths-of-AI-native-companies), with human approval kept wherever an error would be costly. That only works if the company tests and measures what its AI does.

## Can an established company become AI-native?

Not in the strict sense, because an established company was not designed around AI from day one. But it can rebuild specific workflows so AI does the core work. Done across enough of the business, the company operates the way an AI-native one does.

The experts split three ways on this.

### The case against

Cognizant takes the strict view: [existing companies can't become AI-native businesses](https://www.cognizant.com/us/en/aem-i/how-to-think-and-act-like-an-ai-native-business), which it calls logically impossible. It recommends that established firms study how AI-native companies operate and adapt their own AI strategy instead.

### AI-first as the realistic goal

Thoughtworks argues that for most established organizations, AI-first is the more realistic ambition. An AI-first organization [deliberately redesigns its strategy, workflows and operating model to take advantage of AI](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-au/insights/articles/path-to-ai-first-organization), without having been built around it. The reason it gives: established companies run a mix of legacy systems, software products and older tools that cannot all change at the same pace.

### Transformation is possible, but rare

Gartner sees routes in for existing enterprises. It lists [transforming the whole company, starting with single business units, launching spinoffs or acquiring AI-native companies](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/ai-native). Bain describes the AI-native enterprise as a destination established companies can reach, but notes that [most companies are pursuing AI transformation, although few are AI-native enterprises](https://www.bain.com/insights/what-is-an-ai-native-enterprise/), largely because of how their programs are run.

### My answer

The label belongs to companies born around AI. The operating model does not. You cannot go back and found your company differently. You can take one workflow at a time and rebuild it so AI does the routine work, people own the decisions, and results are measured. A 50-person company that has done that for intake, reporting and customer questions runs much more like an AI-native company than like its old self.

That is a more useful goal than the label. It also matches what Bain found holds companies back: [pilots and incremental tools spread across dozens of initiatives](https://www.bain.com/insights/what-is-an-ai-native-enterprise/), instead of real workflow redesign in a few areas.

## What does an AI-native company look like day to day?

McKinsey's interviews give a picture of daily life inside these companies. Examples from [The seven operating truths of AI-native companies](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-seven-operating-truths-of-AI-native-companies) (June 2026):

- At a seed-stage AI company, every sales call is recorded and transcripts go to a shared workspace, with a weekly digest sent to the team.
- A Series A marketplace CEO said his personal team of AI agents boosted his capacity and effectiveness fivefold.
- A Series D fintech runs ten specialized agents on a submitted product idea at once and gets product requirements within hours.
- At a company building AI for doctors, an email agent drafted a careless reply to a contract negotiation. A human caught it before it was sent.

The last example matters as much as the others. AI-native companies are not careless with AI. They decide where people must review the work.

The same pattern fits a services business. Picture a roofing company where every inbound lead is read by AI, sorted by job type and urgency, filed in the CRM with a drafted reply, and turned into a task for the right crew. Staff handle the emergencies and the conversations. I built [a demo of exactly that intake flow](/work/roofing-lead-intake/), and it sorted every message in its test set into the right category.

## AI-native vs AI-enabled: what is the difference?

AI-enabled companies add AI to work that already exists. AI-native companies design the work around AI. Kore.ai puts the line simply: an AI-enabled product [still works without the AI](https://www.kore.ai/blog/what-is-ai-native-organization-benefits-examples), while an AI-native one stops functioning without it.

| | AI-enabled | AI-native |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where AI sits | Added to existing work | Designed into the work |
| If the AI goes down | Work slows | Work stops |
| Typical first step | Licenses and training | Redesigning one workflow |
| What makes it work | Individual skill | Documented processes and data |

For the full comparison, including AI-assisted and AI-first, read [AI-native vs AI-enabled vs AI-first](/blog/ai-native-vs-ai-enabled/).

## Where should a company start?

Start with one workflow that is high in volume, clear in its rules and easy to measure. Rebuild it around AI, test it, and expand from there. Company-wide AI programs tend to spread effort thin.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is an AI-native company?**
An AI-native company is one whose products, workflows and operating model are designed around AI, so AI does core work rather than assisting with it. If you switched the AI off, important parts of the business would stop.

**Is AI-native the same as AI-first?**
No. Thoughtworks defines an AI-native organization as one designed around AI from the outset, and an AI-first organization as one that deliberately redesigns its strategy, workflows and operating model to take advantage of AI. AI-first is the path open to companies that already exist.

**Does a company need to sell an AI product to be AI-native?**
No. A company can run its operations on AI while selling an ordinary product or service. A roofing company whose leads are sorted, routed and followed up by AI runs AI-native operations even though it sells roofs.

**Can an established business become AI-native?**
Not in the strict sense, because it was not designed around AI from day one. But it can rebuild specific workflows so AI does the core work, one function at a time. Done across enough of the business, the result operates the way an AI-native company does.

**Is using ChatGPT enough to make a company AI-native?**
No. Individual employees using a chat assistant makes a company AI-assisted. AI-native means the workflows themselves are designed around AI, with company data, clear ownership and measurement built in.

**How long does it take to become AI-native?**
It is a series of projects, not a single deadline. A first workflow is a small, contained project. Rebuilding several functions takes longer and depends on how well your processes and data are documented.
