Generative engine optimization is how you make sure AI assistants name your business when your customers ask them for help. This guide covers what GEO is, what the research says works, how it differs from SEO and AEO, and a process you can run.

## What is generative engine optimization?

Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the practice of improving how often and how accurately AI systems mention and cite your content in their answers. A generative engine is a search tool that uses a large language model to gather sources and write a single answer, instead of returning a list of links.

The researchers who named the field describe GEO as a black-box optimization framework for content creators. You cannot see inside ChatGPT or Gemini, so you change your content, test, and measure whether your visibility in their answers improves.

## Where did the term GEO come from?

GEO comes from the paper [GEO: Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3) by Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan and Ameet Deshpande. It was first posted to arXiv in November 2023 and accepted to KDD 2024, a major data mining conference.

The team built GEO-bench, a benchmark of user queries across many domains with web sources for each, and tested how different content changes affected whether a source showed up in AI-generated answers.

## What does the research say works?

The paper tested nine content changes, including:

| Method | What it changes |
| --- | --- |
| Cite Sources | Adds citations to credible sources |
| Quotation Addition | Adds relevant quotations from credible sources |
| Statistics Addition | Replaces qualitative claims with numbers |
| Authoritative | Makes the tone more persuasive and authoritative |
| Fluency Optimization | Improves how smoothly the text reads |
| Easy-to-Understand | Simplifies the language |
| Keyword Stuffing | Adds more query keywords, as in old-style SEO |
| Unique Words / Technical Terms | Adds distinctive or technical vocabulary |

Overall, the authors report that GEO can boost visibility by up to 40% in generative engine responses, and that the effect varies by domain.

The most useful finding for smaller sites is in the ranking breakdown. Citing sources lifted visibility by 115.1% for websites ranked fifth in search results. Quotation Addition lifted it by 99.7% and Statistics Addition by 97.9% at the same rank. For the top-ranked site, the same methods reduced visibility. The authors frame this as GEO helping lower-ranked sites compete.

Two cautions. This is one study, run on the generative engines available at the time. And the assistants change often. Treat these findings as a strong starting hypothesis and test them on your own priority questions.

## Why does GEO matter now?

GEO matters because AI answers increasingly sit between your customer and your website. In Pew Research Center's [analysis of March 2025 browsing data](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), about 18% of Google searches produced an AI summary. Users who saw one clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits, compared with 15% for users who did not. Clicks on links inside the summary happened in just 1% of visits.

Pew also found users ended their browsing session on 26% of pages with an AI summary, versus 16% of pages with only traditional results. If the answer itself names a competitor, many people never get to your site to compare.

## How is GEO different from SEO and AEO?

GEO, SEO and AEO share a foundation and differ in what they aim at.

| | SEO | AEO | GEO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Rank pages in search results | Get content pulled into answers for specific questions | Get the brand mentioned and cited in AI answers overall |
| Unit of success | Position and clicks | Answer inclusion | Mentions, citations and how you are described |
| Main levers | Relevance, links, technical health | Direct answers, question headings, structured data | Sourced claims, entity consistency, third-party coverage |
| Measured in | Search Console, analytics | Question-level checks | Question-level checks across assistants, AI referrals, branded search |

## How to do generative engine optimization: a step-by-step process

### 1. Choose the questions that matter

List 20 to 50 questions your buyers ask before they hire or buy. Include comparison questions ("best X for Y"), cost questions and problem questions. These become your tracking set.

### 2. Record a baseline

Ask each question in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google (with AI Overviews). Record whether you are cited, mentioned without a link, or absent, and which competitors and sources appear instead.

### 3. Fix access and the technical basics

Make sure AI crawlers can reach your pages, important content renders in the HTML, and your pages are indexed. This is standard [technical SEO](/services/technical-seo/), and nothing else works without it.

### 4. Rewrite key pages to be quotable

Open each page with a direct answer. Use question-style headings. Keep answer paragraphs short and self-contained; I use a [40-word rule](/blog/the-40-word-rule-how-to-structure-paragraphs-for-ai-citations-in-2026/) for this. Then apply the methods with research behind them: cite credible sources, add specific statistics, and quote experts where it helps the reader.

### 5. Make your entity consistent

Describe your business the same way everywhere: your site, structured data, directory profiles and social profiles. Use Organization, Person and Service schema that ties those profiles together.

### 6. Earn mentions on the pages AI systems read

Look at which sources the assistants cite for your questions in step 2. Those are often list articles, directories, review sites and forums. Getting accurately included there is often the fastest way into the answer.

### 7. Re-check on a schedule

Run the same question set monthly. Track citations and mentions by assistant, AI referral traffic in analytics, and branded search in Search Console. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is generative engine optimization?**
Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the practice of improving how often and how accurately AI systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews mention and cite your content in their answers.

**Who coined the term generative engine optimization?**
The term comes from a research paper by Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari and colleagues from Princeton University and IIT Delhi, first posted in November 2023 and published at the KDD 2024 conference.

**Is GEO different from SEO?**
GEO builds on SEO. SEO aims to rank pages in a list of results. GEO aims to get your content selected, quoted and cited inside an AI-generated answer. Crawlable, well-ranked content is still the starting point for both.

**What is the difference between GEO and AEO?**
The terms overlap and are often used interchangeably. AEO usually refers to getting specific content pulled into answers for specific questions. GEO is often used more broadly for how AI systems describe and recommend a brand.

**Which GEO tactics have research behind them?**
In the original GEO study, adding citations to credible sources, adding relevant quotations and adding statistics were among the methods that improved visibility in generative engine responses. The effect varied by domain and by how highly a page already ranked.

**How do you measure GEO results?**
Pick a fixed set of priority questions, check which AI assistants mention or cite you for each one on a regular schedule, and track AI referral traffic and branded search alongside Search Console data.
